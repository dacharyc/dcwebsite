Randomly just thinking about that time I wrote a tailored iOS/iPadOS app when a really popular game had just launched, emailed all the video game media outlets about it, and it made it to number 1 in its category in the App Store for a little bit. (No particular reason. No parallels to rushing an app right now related to another game release or anything like that.)

Some company loved it and reached out to me to set up a call. They wanted me to commit to build a mod, which would be some sort of overlay, that would go over the game and use my app's data models and concepts. I think they said I would get compensated through some sort of profit share from their subscriber revenue.

I said no. I wasn't a PC gamer at the time; only consoles from 2006 until early 2026; and I wasn't their audience so I wasn't interested in building it just for the money.

Looking back, I don't think I gave myself enough credit for saying no to that. I have been very fortunate to work on stuff I actually enjoy working on for the last decade. I think that has been the key to staving off burnout in spite of the hours I put in at work and outside of work on all my various projects. It's all stuff I like doing.

So I guess my takeaway here is to chase things that you enjoy, and don't be afraid to say no to things you don't. Saying no makes room for things you *do* enjoy.
